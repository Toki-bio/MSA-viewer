ViewAlign examples

Each link opens the viewer with a small alignment and one tool already run, and a note that says what you are looking at. Everything stays in your browser. To go back to an empty viewer, open ViewAlign; the manual explains every tool.

Synthetic alignments with a known answer

These sequences are invented. Each was made with a property planted in it, so you can see whether the tool finds it. The script that makes them and the answers are in the examples/gallery folder (make_gallery.js, truth.json).

Subfamilies: grouping by shared columns

30 copies of three invented subfamilies (sfA, sfB, sfC), 10 each, in mixed order. Each subfamily has its own base at 12 columns, and every copy has about 3 % changes of its own.

Shown: Clustering › Diagnostic SNPs › Find SNP groups with the default thresholds. The three subfamilies get one colour each, found from the sequences alone. A group needs at least Min Features (5) columns that all its copies share and no other copy has.

Open example subfamilies.fa

Tree: a known tree recovered

Nine sequences of 600 bases copied down a known tree with random changes: two clades of four, ((X1,X2),(X3,X4)) and ((Y1,Y2),(Y3,Y4)), and a distant Out.

Shown: Actions › Tree, neighbour-joining from p-distances, rooted on Out. Compare it with the true tree in tree_true.nwk.

Open example tree.fa tree_true.nwk

Codons: synonymous, missense, stop, frameshift

A 40-codon reading frame and five copies, each named after its one change: a synonymous change, a missense change (Ala to Glu), an early stop, a one-base loss that shifts the frame, and a whole lost codon that keeps it.

Shown: Display › Codon analysis. Codon positions are coloured and each change is marked where it was made.

Open example codons.fa

Amino acids: the same copies translated

The coding sequences above, shown as amino acids.

Shown: Display › Codon analysis with AA only. The missense copy has E at codon 8, the early stop ends the protein at codon 15, and after its lost base the frameshift copy reads a different protein.

Consensus and conservation shading

20 rows in zones where a set number of rows share each column's main base: all 20, 16 (80 %), 12 (60 %), 7 (35 %), an even A/G split, and a stretch where only 4 rows have a base.

Shown: the default shading (black, dark and light grey at 90, 70 and 50 %) and the consensus line, with Type set to Ambiguous so the A/G columns read R. Move the Shade and Consensus sliders to see the zones change.
